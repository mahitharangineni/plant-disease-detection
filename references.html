Annotated Bibliography

The theoretical foundations, experimental architectures, and empirical benchmarks presented in this tutorial are derived from peer-reviewed computer vision literature. Below is the annotated bibliography detailing the five core references, their methodological contributions, and their specific utility to agricultural computer vision systems.

[1] Ding et al. (2024)

W. Ding, M. Abdel-Basset, I. Alrashdi, and H. Hawash, “Next generation of computer vision for plant disease monitoring in precision agriculture: A contemporary survey, taxonomy, experiments, and future direction,” Information Sciences, vol. 665, article 120338, 2024.

Synopsis: This survey provides a comprehensive contemporary taxonomy of modern computer vision techniques applied to precision agriculture, examining deep convolutional neural networks, YOLO object detection models, vision transformers (ViTs), and lightweight edge networks. It discusses key real-world challenges including extreme illumination changes, background foliage clutter, and low-latency edge deployment.

Why this source is useful: Establishes the foundational computer vision taxonomy and framing for this tutorial, justifying the transition from standard image classification to spatial object detection and lightweight edge deployment in agricultural settings.

View Journal Source (DOI: 10.1016/j.ins.2024.120338) →

[2] Iftikhar et al. (2024)

M. Iftikhar, I. A. Kandhro, N. Kausar, et al., “Plant disease management: a fine-tuned enhanced CNN approach with mobile app integration for early detection and classification,” Artificial Intelligence Review, vol. 57, article 167, 2024.

Synopsis: This study develops a fine-tuned convolutional neural network architecture coupled with transfer learning for automated crop disease diagnosis, integrated into a functional smartphone application. The authors evaluate real-world crop pathologies (apple, corn, and potato) and analyze edge inference efficiency.

Why this source is useful: Directly informs the mobile image capture, CNN transfer learning, and on-device deployment modules of this tutorial, validating practical smartphone integration for field farmers.

View Journal Source (DOI: 10.1007/s10462-024-10809-z) →

[3] Javidan et al. (2024)

S. M. Javidan, A. Banakar, K. Rahnama, K. Asefpour Vakilian, and Y. Ampatzidis, “Feature engineering to identify plant diseases using image processing and artificial intelligence: A comprehensive review,” Smart Agricultural Technology, vol. 8, article 100480, 2024.

Synopsis: A comprehensive technical investigation into morphological, textural (GLCM), and chromatic feature representations used to identify foliar plant diseases. The paper details image preprocessing routines, color space transformations (HSV, CIE $L^*a^*b^*$), and disease-area segmentation methodologies.

Why this source is useful: Underpins the image preprocessing, color space conversion, and visual feature extraction sections of this tutorial, providing the mathematical rationale for decoupling luminance from chrominance in outdoor photography.

View Journal Source (DOI: 10.1016/j.atech.2024.100480) →

[4] Leite et al. (2024)

D. Leite, A. Brito, and G. Faccioli, “Advancements and outlooks in utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks for plant disease severity assessment: A comprehensive review,” Smart Agricultural Technology, vol. 9, article 100573, 2024.

Synopsis: This review examines how deep learning and semantic segmentation models transcend categorical disease classification to perform quantitative disease severity assessment. It analyzes mathematical severity indices, pixel-level loss functions, and automated phenotyping benchmarks.

Why this source is useful: Formulates the theoretical foundation for Module 4 (Segmentation & Severity Estimation), justifying the quantitative percentage severity formula: $\text{Severity} = (\text{Diseased Pixels} / \text{Total Leaf Pixels}) \times 100$.

View Journal Source (DOI: 10.1016/j.atech.2024.100573) →

[5] Montagut Marques et al. (2025)

M. J. Montagut Marques, L. Mingxin, K. T. Shiojiri, T. Hagiwara, K. Hirose, K. Shiojiri, and S. Umezu, “Plant Doctor: A hybrid machine learning and image segmentation software to quantify plant damage in video footage,” Measurement, vol. 249, article 117094, 2025.

Synopsis: Introduces the Plant Doctor framework—a multi-stage computer vision system combining YOLOv8 for spatial leaf detection, DeepSORT for multi-frame temporal leaf tracking, and DeepLabV3+ for pixel-level damage segmentation across continuous video. The authors report experimental benchmarks ($0.99$ mAP@0.5 for YOLO, $0.96$ leaf IoU, $0.76$ damage IoU) alongside the critical limitation that a 1-minute video required approximately 50 minutes of processing time.

Why this source is useful: Serves as the central case study (Module 6) and primary motivation for multi-view video tracking (Module 5), providing concrete real-world empirical performance numbers and highlighting current computational bottlenecks in video-based precision agriculture.

View Journal Source (DOI: 10.1016/j.measurement.2025.117094) →
